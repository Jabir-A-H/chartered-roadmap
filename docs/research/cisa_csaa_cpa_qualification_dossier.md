## Executive Summary & Taxonomy Overview

Modern professional accountancy and assurance have expanded far beyond classical single-entity statutory bookkeeping. Today's global corporate and regulatory landscape demands specialized, cross-functional fluency across three critical pillars:
1. **Information Systems & Cybersecurity Assurance (CISA):** Safeguarding digital infrastructure, enterprise algorithms, cloud architectures, and operational resilience.
2. **Islamic Financial Jurisprudence & Ethical Governance (CSAA):** Upholding Shari'ah compliance, multi-trillion-dollar Islamic capital market instruments (*Sukuk*), asset-backed financing, and ethical fiduciary audit across 45+ jurisdictions.
3. **Public Accountancy & Strategic Advisory (Uniform CPA Evolution):** The reimagined gold standard of US public accounting combining foundational core competencies (audit, financial accounting, regulation) with agile specialization in systems controls (ISC), technical analysis (BAR), or advanced tax planning (TCP).

# SECTION 1: ISACA CISA (Certified Information Systems Auditor)

## 1.1 Credential Profile & Global Authority
- **Certifying Body:** Information Systems Audit and Control Association (ISACA), Schaumburg, Illinois, USA.
- **Global Recognition:** ANSI/ISO/IEC 17024 accredited; recognized worldwide as the premier benchmark for professionals who audit, control, monitor, and assess an organization's information technology and business systems.
- **Current Syllabus Edition:** CISA 2024–2025 Job Practice (Effective August 1, 2024).
- **Official Portal:** [https://www.isaca.org/credentialing/cisa](https://www.isaca.org/credentialing/cisa)

## 1.2 Examination Architecture & Assessment Parameters
- **Question Format:** 150 Multiple-Choice Questions (MCQs), each with four answer choices and a single best answer. Questions are scenario-based and evaluate knowledge application, diagnostic reasoning, and situational risk evaluation.
- **Duration:** 4 Hours (240 minutes) continuous testing.
- **Scoring Methodology:** Scaled scoring system ranging from **200 to 800**. 
  - Passing Score: **450** (represents minimum standard of competence established by ISACA's CISA Certification Working Group).
  - Unscored Pretest Items: A subset of questions are un-scored trial items included for statistical calibration.
- **Delivery Mode:** Computer-Based Testing (CBT) administered globally via Prometric test centers or online remotely proctored delivery.

### Domain 1: Information Systems Auditing Process (18%)
*Objective: Provide audit services in accordance with IT audit standards to assist organizations in protecting and controlling information systems.*

#### Sub-Domain A: Planning
1. **IS Audit Standards, Guidelines, and Codes of Ethics:**
   - Adherence to ISACA's ITAF (Information Technology Assurance Framework).
   - Code of Professional Ethics, independence, objectivity, and professional skepticism.
   - Regulatory requirements, legal mandates, and cross-border data sovereignty laws.
2. **Types of Audits, Assessments, and Reviews:**
   - Financial audits, operational audits, integrated audits, compliance audits, forensic investigations, and third-party vendor audits (SOC 1, SOC 2, SOC 3).
3. **Risk-Based Audit Planning:**
   - Enterprise risk assessment methodologies, audit universe definition, risk ranking, material risk identification, and annual audit plan formulation.
4. **Types of Controls & Internal Control Frameworks:**
   - Entity-level controls, IT general controls (ITGC), application controls.
   - Preventive, detective, and corrective controls; compensating controls.
   - Mapping to COSO Internal Control—Integrated Framework and COBIT 2019.

#### Sub-Domain B: Execution
1. **Audit Project Management:**
   - Engagement planning, milestone scheduling, audit resource management, and workpaper administration.
2. **Audit Testing and Sampling Methodology:**
   - Statistical sampling (attribute, variable, discovery) vs. non-statistical sampling.
   - Tolerable error rates, confidence levels, and sampling risk.
3. **Audit Evidence Collection Techniques:**
   - Inquiry, observation, inspection, re-performance, and external confirmation.
   - Chain of custody, evidentiary integrity, and electronic evidence preservation.
4. **Audit Data Analytics (ADA) & Continuous Auditing:**
   - Computer-Assisted Audit Techniques (CAATs), SQL interrogation, automated anomaly detection, population analysis, and continuous monitoring feeds.
5. **Reporting and Communication Techniques:**
   - Drafting audit findings using the 5 C's: Condition, Criteria, Cause, Consequence (Effect), and Corrective Action (Recommendation).
   - Executive exit conferences, management response tracking, and audit committee presentations.
6. **Quality Assurance and Improvement Program (QAIP):**
   - Post-audit reviews, peer reviews, and ongoing process improvement.

### Domain 2: Governance and Management of IT (18%)
*Objective: Confirm the organization has necessary leadership, organizational structures, and processes to support and sustain corporate strategy and objectives.*

#### Sub-Domain A: IT Governance
1. **IT Governance & Strategy Alignment:**
   - Aligning digital capabilities with enterprise strategy; IT steering committees and Board of Directors fiduciary duties.
2. **IT-Related Frameworks & Standards:**
   - COBIT 2019 (Governance & Management Objectives), ITIL 4, TOGAF, ISO/IEC 38500.
3. **IT Standards, Policies, and Procedures:**
   - Architecture of hierarchical governance documents: Policy $\rightarrow$ Standard $\rightarrow$ Guideline $\rightarrow$ Procedure.
4. **Organizational Structure & Segregation of Duties (SoD):**
   - Segregation between development, operations, security, and quality assurance.
   - Compensating controls for small IT environments and privileged user access matrices.
5. **Enterprise Architecture (EA):**
   - Strategic blueprint spanning business architecture, information architecture, applications architecture, and technology architecture.
6. **Enterprise Risk Management (ERM):**
   - Risk appetite, risk tolerance, Key Risk Indicators (KRIs), risk heat maps, and mitigation strategies (avoid, mitigate, transfer, accept).
7. **Privacy Program and Principles:**
   - Global privacy compliance: GDPR, CCPA/CPRA, HIPAA; Privacy by Design; Data Protection Officer (DPO) duties.
8. **Data Governance & Data Lifecycle Management:**
   - Data classification (Public, Internal, Confidential, Restricted), data ownership, custodianship, and retention/destruction standards.

#### Sub-Domain B: IT Management
1. **IT Resource Management:**
   - Human capital management, capacity forecasting, and skills inventory.
2. **IT Service Provider Acquisition and Management (TPRM):**
   - Third-Party Risk Management (TPRM), Request for Proposal (RFP) processes, Service Level Agreements (SLAs), Operational Level Agreements (OLAs), and right-to-audit clauses.
3. **IT Performance Monitoring and Reporting:**
   - Balanced Scorecards (BSC), Key Performance Indicators (KPIs), and executive dashboards.
4. **Quality Assurance and Quality Management (QA/QM):**
   - ISO 9001 quality management systems, total quality management, and software testing maturity.

### Domain 3: Information Systems Acquisition, Development & Implementation (12%)
*Objective: Provide assurance that the practices for the acquisition, development, testing, and implementation of information systems meet enterprise objectives.*

#### Sub-Domain A: Acquisition and Development
1. **Project Governance and Management:**
   - Project management offices (PMO), project charter, project life cycle, work breakdown structures (WBS), and critical path methodology (CPM).
2. **Business Case and Feasibility Analysis:**
   - Cost-benefit analysis, Return on Investment (ROI), Net Present Value (NPV), and economic/technical/operational feasibility.
3. **System Development Methodologies:**
   - Traditional Waterfall, Agile (Scrum/Kanban), Rapid Application Development (RAD), and DevSecOps pipelines.
4. **Control Identification and Design:**
   - Embedding security and compliance controls into the software development lifecycle (SDLC); input, processing, and output controls.

#### Sub-Domain B: Implementation
1. **System Readiness and Implementation Testing:**
   - Unit testing, integration testing, system testing, User Acceptance Testing (UAT), regression testing, stress/load testing, and security penetration testing.
2. **Implementation Configuration and Release Management:**
   - Code repositories, version control, build automation, rollback plans, and deployment pipelines.
3. **System Migration, Infrastructure Deployment, and Cutover Strategies:**
   - Cutover strategies: Parallel run, phased rollout, pilot transition, and direct cutover (cold turkey/flash cut).
   - Data migration validation: Data cleansing, field mapping, hash total checks, and record count reconciliations.
4. **Post-Implementation Review (PIR):**
   - Evaluating whether projected ROI, operational objectives, and security controls were achieved post-go-live.

### Domain 4: Information Systems Operations and Business Resilience (26%)
*Objective: Provide assurance that IT operational processes, service delivery, and resilience mechanisms support business needs and maintain continuous processing.*

#### Sub-Domain A: IT Operations
1. **IT Asset Management (ITAM):**
   - Hardware and software inventory tracking, configuration management databases (CMDB), and license compliance audits.
2. **Job Scheduling and Production Process Automation:**
   - Automated batch scheduling, dependency mapping, and job failure exception handling.
3. **System Interfaces & Application Programming Interfaces (APIs):**
   - Middleware architecture, API gateway security, webhook verification, and ETL data pipelines.
4. **Shadow IT & End-User Computing (EUC):**
   - Identifying unauthorized SaaS usage, unmanaged user-developed spreadsheets, and local desktop databases; implementing compensating access and reconciliation controls.
5. **Systems Availability & Capacity Management:**
   - CPU, memory, bandwidth, and storage capacity forecasting; high availability clustering and load balancing.
6. **Problem and Incident Management:**
   - Incident handling (ITIL Incident Management) vs. root-cause analysis (Problem Management); known error databases (KEDB).
7. **IT Change, Configuration, Release, and Patch Management:**
   - Change Advisory Boards (CAB), emergency change procedures, configuration baselines, and systematic patch regression testing.
8. **Operational Log Management:**
   - Centralized Security Information and Event Management (SIEM), log retention policies, time synchronization (NTP), and immutable write-once audit logs.
9. **IT Service Level Management:**
   - Monitoring uptime percentages, mean time between failures (MTBF), and mean time to repair (MTTR).
10. **Database Management & Maintenance:**
    - Relational vs. NoSQL architectures, database indexing, deadlocks, table partitioning, and database administrator (DBA) privileged monitoring.

#### Sub-Domain B: Business Resilience
1. **Business Impact Analysis (BIA):**
   - Critical business process identification, Maximum Tolerable Downtime (MTD), Recovery Time Objective (RTO), and Recovery Point Objective (RPO).
2. **Business Continuity Plan (BCP) & Disaster Recovery Plan (DRP):**
   - Emergency response procedures, crisis communication plans, and alternate operating facility arrangements.
3. **Backup, Restore, and Redundancy Measures:**
   - Full, differential, and incremental backups; 3-2-1 backup rule; immutable storage (WORM); snapshots; and cold, warm, hot, and cloud-native recovery sites.
4. **Resilience Testing & Drills:**
   - Tabletop exercises, walk-through tests, simulation tests, parallel tests, and full-interruption cutover tests.

### Domain 5: Protection of Information Assets (26%)
*Objective: Provide assurance that the organization's security policies, standards, procedures, and controls ensure the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of information assets.*

#### Sub-Domain A: Information Asset Security and Control
1. **Information Asset Security Frameworks & Standards:**
   - ISO/IEC 27001 / 27002, NIST Cybersecurity Framework (NIST CSF 2.0), Center for Internet Security (CIS) Controls.
2. **Privacy Principles & PII Protection:**
   - Personally Identifiable Information (PII) minimization, pseudonymization, data encryption at rest and in transit, and tokenization.
3. **Physical Access & Environmental Controls:**
   - Perimeter fencing, biometrics, multi-factor mantrap airlocks, CCTV retention, clean agent fire suppression (FM-200 / Inergen), HVAC environmental sensors, and redundant Uninterruptible Power Supplies (UPS) / diesel generators.
4. **Identity and Access Management (IAM):**
   - Principle of Least Privilege, Need-to-Know, Role-Based Access Control (RBAC), Attribute-Based Access Control (ABAC), Privileged Access Management (PAM), Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA), and Single Sign-On (SSO) with SAML/OIDC.
5. **Network and Endpoint Security:**
   - Zero Trust Architecture (ZTA), Next-Generation Firewalls (NGFW), Intrusion Detection/Prevention Systems (IDS/IPS), Virtual Private Networks (VPN), Demilitarized Zones (DMZ), Network Access Control (NAC), and Endpoint Detection and Response (EDR/XDR).
6. **Data Encryption and Cryptography:**
   - Symmetric encryption (AES-256), Asymmetric encryption (RSA, ECC), cryptographic hashing algorithms (SHA-256, SHA-3), Public Key Infrastructure (PKI), digital certificates, certificate authorities (CA), and Hardware Security Modules (HSM).
7. **Threat and Vulnerability Management:**
   - Vulnerability scanning engines, manual penetration testing (black-box, white-box, gray-box), threat intelligence feeds, Common Vulnerability Scoring System (CVSS), and zero-day threat mitigation.
8. **Security Incident Handling, Response, and Forensics:**
   - Incident response lifecycle: Preparation, Detection & Analysis, Containment, Eradication, Recovery, and Post-Incident Lessons Learned.
   - Digital forensics principles: Order of volatility, write blockers, bit-stream disk imaging, and strict chain of custody.
9. **Emerging Technology Security Considerations:**
   - Cloud service models (SaaS, PaaS, IaaS) and deployment models; Cloud Shared Responsibility Model.
   - Containerization security (Docker, Kubernetes), Serverless computing risks, Artificial Intelligence / Machine Learning model poisoning, and Internet of Things (IoT) edge risks.

## 1.4 Professional Experience, Waivers, and Maintenance
- **5-Year Work Experience Requirement:**
  - Candidates must submit verified evidence of a minimum of **5 years** of professional work experience in information systems auditing, control, assurance, or security.
  - Work experience must be gained within the 10-year period preceding the application date or within 5 years after passing the exam.
- **Substitution Waivers (Up to a maximum of 3 years):**
  - **1 Year Waiver:** 1 full year of non-IS audit experience OR 1 full year of general IT work experience OR an associate degree (60 college credit hours).
  - **2 Year Waiver:** A bachelor's degree (120 college credit hours) from an accredited institution.
  - **3 Year Waiver:** A master's degree in information security, information technology, computer science, or related discipline OR 2 years of full-time university instructor experience in a related field.
- **Continuous Professional Education (CPE):**
  - **120 CPE hours** over each 3-year reporting cycle.
  - Minimum of **20 CPE hours** annually.
  - Payment of annual maintenance fees and strict adherence to the ISACA Code of Professional Ethics.

# SECTION 2: AAOIFI CSAA (Certified Shari'ah Advisor and Auditor)

## 2.1 Credential Profile & Global Authority
- **Certifying Body:** Accounting and Auditing Organization for Islamic Financial Institutions (AAOIFI), Manama, Kingdom of Bahrain.
- **Global Recognition:** Recognized by central banks, monetary authorities, and Islamic financial institutions across 45+ jurisdictions (including GCC, Southeast Asia, South Asia, UK, and Africa) as the definitive professional fellowship qualification for Islamic finance compliance and Shari'ah governance.
- **Current Syllabus Edition:** Revamped Modular Architecture (Two Standalone Certificates of Proficiency).
- **Official Portal:** [https://aaoifi.com](https://aaoifi.com)

  1. **CPSS:** Certificate of Proficiency in Shari'ah Standards
  2. **CPAAGE:** Certificate of Proficiency in Audit, Assurance, Governance, and Ethics

- **Duration:** 3 Hours (180 minutes) per module.
- **Passing Benchmark:** Typically **70%** (ranging between 60% and 70% based on specific module statistical scaling policies).
- **Delivery Mode:** Computer-Based Examination (CBE) delivered via authorized international partner centers or proctored online testing.
- **Stand-Alone Portability:** Passing each module yields an independent official Certificate of Proficiency. Passing both modules completes the academic requirement for the full CSAA Fellowship.

## 2.3 Detailed Syllabus Breakdown: Module 1 — CPSS (Certificate of Proficiency in Shari'ah Standards)

The CPSS curriculum covers roughly **60 active AAOIFI Shari'ah Standards (SS)**, ensuring comprehensive operational and contractual competence.

### Part A: Foundational Prohibitions & Principles of Islamic Commercial Law
1. **The Three Core Prohibitions:**
   - **Riba (Usury/Interest):** Distinction between *Riba al-Duyun* (debt-related interest), *Riba al-Jahiliyyah*, *Riba al-Fadl* (exchange of unequal quantities of ribawi commodities: gold, silver, wheat, barley, dates, salt), and *Riba al-Nasi'ah* (delay in exchange).
   - **Gharar (Excessive Uncertainty/Ambiguity):** Minor vs. substantial gharar (*Gharar Fahish*); conditions invalidating contracts. Detailed rules under **SS No. 31: Controls on Gharar in Financial Transactions**.
   - **Maysir & Qimar (Gambling & Pure Speculation):** Prohibition of binary financial bets and unbacked speculative exposure.
2. **Possession (*Qabd*) & Risk Transfer:**
   - Detailed rules under **SS No. 18: Possession (Qabd)**: Actual physical possession (*Qabd Haqiqi*) vs. constructive/legal possession (*Qabd Hukmi*).
   - Invariant: *"No profit without bearing liability/risk (*Al-kharaj bi al-daman* / *Al-ghunm bi al-ghurm*)"*.
3. **General Contract Principles & Fiqh Mechanics:**
   - **SS No. 25: Combination of Contracts** (Permissible multi-contract bundling vs. forbidden interdependent conditional contracts *Bay'atayn fi Bay'ah*).
   - **SS No. 49: Unilateral and Bilateral Promises (*Wa'd* and *Muwa'adah*):** Binding nature of unilateral promises in commercial transactions (*Wa'd Mulzim*).
   - **SS No. 47: Rules for Calculating Profit in Financial Transactions.**
   - **SS No. 42: Financial Rights and How They Are Exercised and Transferred.**

### Part B: Commercial Financing & Trade Contracts
1. **Murabahah to the Purchase Orderer (SS No. 8):**
   - Sequence of execution: Order request $\rightarrow$ Promise to purchase (*Wa'd*) $\rightarrow$ Bank acquires ownership from supplier $\rightarrow$ Possession (*Qabd*) $\rightarrow$ Sale contract execution to client at cost-plus markup $\rightarrow$ Installment repayment.
   - Prohibited practices: Client acting as direct agent without physical transfer; rollovers of debt; compound penalty charges (penalties must be directed exclusively to charity).
2. **Salam & Parallel Salam (SS No. 10):**
   - Forward sale of fungible goods with full upfront cash payment and deferred physical delivery.
   - Parallel Salam mechanics: Maintaining absolute decoupling between the purchase Salam and the sales Salam.
3. **Istisna'a & Parallel Istisna'a (SS No. 11):**
   - Manufacturing/construction contract where goods are manufactured to precise custom specifications.
   - Flexibility in progress payment installments; application in real estate and infrastructure development.
4. **Tawarruq & Commodity Murabahah (SS No. 30):**
   - Monetization structure: Genuine international commodity transactions (LME, Bursa Suq Al-Sila) vs. organized/synthetic *Tawarruq Munazzam*.
   - Strict AAOIFI conditions prohibiting agency loops where the bank sells back to the original supplier (*Bay' al-'Inah*).
5. **Foreign Exchange & Currency Trading (SS No. 1: Sarf):**
   - Immediate spot possession requirement; prohibition of forward forex contracts and deferral of settlement.
6. **Gold and Its Trading Parameters (SS No. 57):**
   - Parameters for physical and digital gold trading; vaulting and ownership allocation.

### Part C: Leasing & Service Contracts
1. **Ijarah and Ijarah Muntahia Bittamleek (SS No. 9):**
   - Operating lease (*Ijarah Tashghiliyyah*) vs. Lease ending with transfer of ownership (*Ijarah Muntahia Bittamleek*).
   - Allocation of ownership risks: Major maintenance, structural insurance (*Takaful*), and total asset destruction remain with the lessor (bank); routine maintenance with the lessee.
   - Mechanisms of title transfer: Independent gift contract (*Hibah*) or separate nominal sale contract at end of lease term.
2. **Agency / Wakalah (SS No. 23) & Investment Agency (*Wakalah bi al-Istithmar*):**
   - Principal-agent relationship; fixed agency fee (*Ujrah*) vs. performance incentive fees for surplus returns over an anticipated hurdle benchmark.
3. **Hiring of Persons & Services (SS No. 34) & Ju'alah (SS No. 15):**
   - Service agreements, unilateral reward contracts (*Ju'alah*) for achieving specific results.

### Part D: Partnerships & Equity Financing
1. **Mudarabah (SS No. 13):**
   - Trust-based partnership between capital provider (*Rabb al-Mal*) and managing entrepreneur (*Mudarib*).
   - Profit distribution strictly by pre-agreed ratios; financial loss borne exclusively by the capital provider (unless due to Mudarib's proven negligence, misconduct, or breach of contract).
   - Prohibition of capital guarantees or fixed lump-sum profit commitments by the Mudarib.
2. **Musharakah & Diminishing Musharakah (SS No. 12):**
   - Joint venture partnership where all partners contribute capital and share profits according to agreed ratios and losses strictly according to capital contribution ratios.
   - Diminishing Musharakah (*Musharakah Mutanaqisah*): Bank and client co-own an asset (e.g., home); client leases bank's share (Ijarah) and progressively purchases bank's ownership units until 100% ownership transfers.

### Part E: Capital Markets, Social Finance & Contemporary Instruments
1. **Investment Sukuk (SS No. 17):**
   - Asset-backed vs. asset-based Sukuk; undivided pro-rata ownership shares in tangible assets, usufructs, or services.
   - Structural prohibition of purchase undertakings (*Wa'd*) at par value for equity-based Sukuk (Mudarabah/Musharakah Sukuk) at maturity.
2. **Takaful / Islamic Insurance (SS No. 26):**
   - Mutual cooperation (*Ta'awun*) and voluntary donation (*Tabarru'*); complete separation between the Participant Takaful Fund (risk pool owned by policyholders) and the Shareholders' Fund (operated under Wakalah or Mudarabah).
   - Managing underwriting deficits through interest-free benevolent loans (*Qard Hasan*).
3. **Zakah (SS No. 35):**
   - Net assets vs. working capital calculation methods for corporate entities; zakatable assets vs. exempt fixed assets.
4. **Waqf / Islamic Endowments (SS No. 60):**
   - Governance, preservation of principal, and investment of cash waqf portfolios.
5. **Sale of Debt / Bay' al-Dayn (SS No. 59) & Financial Papers (SS No. 21):**
   - Shari'ah parameters on secondary market debt trading; equity screening filters (5% non-compliant revenue, 33% debt-to-market-cap leverage).

## 2.4 Detailed Syllabus Breakdown: Module 2 — CPAAGE (Certificate of Proficiency in Audit, Assurance, Governance, and Ethics)

The CPAAGE module provides the operational machinery to assure that financial institutions operate strictly within AAOIFI mandates.

### Part A: AAOIFI Governance Standards (GS)
1. **GS 1: Shari'ah Supervisory Board (SSB) — Appointment, Composition, and Report:**
   - Minimum board size (at least 3 specialized Shari'ah jurists).
   - Appointment by the General Assembly / Shareholders (not executive management).
   - Mandate: Ex-ante approval of products, policies, and standard contracts; ongoing supervision; ex-post annual audit report.
   - Structure and binding legal authority of the annual SSB report.
2. **GS 2: Shari'ah Review:**
   - Internal procedures conducted by the SSB to examine contracts, transactions, circulars, and financial statements.
3. **GS 3 & GS 11: Internal Shari'ah Review (ISR) & Internal Shari'ah Audit (ISA):**
   - Establishing an independent Internal Shari'ah Audit Department (ISAD).
   - Dual reporting line: Functionally reporting directly to the SSB; administratively to the Board Audit Committee / CEO.
   - Developing risk-based internal Shari'ah audit plans, working papers, sample testing, and remediation tracking.
4. **GS 4: Audit and Governance Committee for Islamic Financial Institutions:**
   - Oversight of internal controls, financial reporting, and alignment between external auditors, internal auditors, and the SSB.
5. **GS 5 & GS 7: Independence of the Shari'ah Supervisory Board:**
   - Mitigating conflicts of interest; rules preventing SSB members from holding executive roles or borrowing from the institution on preferential terms.
6. **GS 10 & GS 13: Shari'ah Compliance and Risk Management:**
   - Identification, measurement, and mitigation of Shari'ah Non-Compliance Risk (SNCR).
   - Accounting and legal treatment of non-compliant income (*purification process*: mandatory routing to charity with no tax or corporate benefit).
7. **GS 16: Institutional Framework for the Implementation of Ethics:**
   - Creating ethical culture, whistleblower protections, and anti-corruption baselines.
8. **GS 22: Application of Shari'ah Governance Principles to Islamic Subsidiaries and Windows:**
   - Governance mandates for conventional banks operating dedicated Islamic banking windows or subsidiary entities.

### Part B: Auditing & Assurance Standards for IFIs (ASIFI)
1. **ASIFI 1: Objective and Principles of Auditing:**
   - Objectives of an audit of financial statements of Islamic financial institutions; compliance with AAOIFI Financial Accounting Standards (FAS) and international standards.
2. **ASIFI 2: The Auditor's Report:**
   - Expressing an audit opinion on whether the financial statements present fairly, in all material respects, the financial position in accordance with AAOIFI FAS and Shari'ah rules.
3. **ASIFI 3: Terms of Audit Engagement:**
   - Drafting engagement letters explicitly referencing AAOIFI standards and access to SSB rulings.
4. **ASIFI 5: Auditor's Responsibility to Consider Fraud and Error:**
   - Assessing risks of fraudulent financial reporting and misappropriation of restricted/unrestricted investment funds.
5. **Substantive Audit Procedures Unique to Islamic Banking:**
   - Verifying genuine asset ownership before execution of sales.
   - Auditing the calculation and allocation of profits between Shareholders and Profit Sharing Investment Account (PSIA) holders.
   - Inspecting Profit Equalization Reserves (PER) and Investment Risk Reserves (IRR) to ensure against artificial profit smoothing.

### Part C: AAOIFI Code of Ethics for Professional Accountants & Shari'ah Advisors
1. **Fundamental Ethical Principles:**
   - Trustworthiness (*Amanah*), Truthfulness (*Sidq*), Justice and Equity (*'Adl*), God-consciousness (*Taqwa*), Professional Competence (*Kafa'ah*), and Confidentiality.
2. **Managing Fiduciary Conflicts:**
   - Protecting the rights of Unrestricted Investment Account Holders (UIAHs) who bear investment risk but lack voting rights at shareholder meetings.

## 2.5 Practical Experience Requirement (PER) & CIPA Upgrades
- **PER Requirement:** Attainment of the full CSAA Fellowship designation requires candidates to complete **1 year of verified practical experience** within an Islamic Financial Institution (IFI), dedicated Islamic banking window, regulatory/central bank Islamic finance division, or accredited accounting/audit firm performing Shari'ah assurance.
- **The CIPA Connection:** Candidates who complete the CSAA (CPSS + CPAAGE) can achieve AAOIFI's premier financial accounting fellowship, the **CIPA (Certified Islamic Professional Accountant)**, by passing one remaining module: **CPFAS (Certificate of Proficiency in Financial Accounting Standards)**.

- **Governing Bodies:** American Institute of Certified Public Accountants (AICPA) and the National Association of State Boards of Accountancy (NASBA).
- **Global Recognition:** The world's preeminent public accounting qualification, granting statutory audit signature rights, tax representation before the IRS, and elite financial leadership standing across 55 US jurisdictions and multinational corporations.
- **Effective Launch Date:** January 10, 2024 (Replaced legacy 4-core CPA Exam).

## 3.3 The 3 Core Sections (Detailed Blueprints)

### 1. AUD: Auditing and Attestation (Core)
*Focus: Professional responsibilities, auditing standards (AICPA SAS, PCAOB, IAASB), assessing risk and developing planned responses, performing procedures, and forming opinions.*
- **Area I: Ethics, Professional Responsibilities and General Principles (15–25%)**
  - AICPA Code of Professional Conduct (Independence, Objectivity, Due Care).
  - SEC, PCAOB, GAO (Yellow Book), and Department of Labor independence rules.
  - Quality management systems for accounting firms (SQMS 1 & 2).
- **Area II: Assessing Risk and Developing a Planned Response (25–35%)**
  - Understanding the entity, its environment, and internal controls (COSO framework).
  - Identifying and assessing material misstatement risks (fraud vs. error).
  - Designing audit programs, materiality baselines, and responses to assessed risks.
- **Area III: Performing Further Procedures and Obtaining Evidence (30–40%)**
  - Tests of controls (ITGC and application controls).
  - Substantive analytical procedures and tests of details.
  - Audit sampling (statistical and non-statistical).
  - Audit documentation and evaluation of audit findings.
- **Area IV: Forming Conclusions and Reporting (10–20%)**
  - Unmodified (clean), qualified, adverse, and disclaimer audit reports.
  - Critical Audit Matters (CAMs - PCAOB) vs. Key Audit Matters (KAMs - IAASB).
  - Reviews (SSARS), Compilations, Agreed-Upon Procedures (SSAE), and SOC reporting fundamentals.

---

### 2. FAR: Financial Accounting and Reporting (Core)
*Focus: US GAAP framework, financial statement preparation, balance sheet and income statement mechanics, revenue recognition, leases, and local government accounting.*
- **Area I: Financial Reporting (30–40%)**
  - Standard financial statements: Balance Sheet, Income Statement, Statement of Comprehensive Income, Statement of Cash Flows (Direct vs. Indirect).
  - Notes disclosures, accounting changes, error corrections, and subsequent events.
  - Interim reporting and segment reporting.
- **Area II: Select Financial Statement Accounts (30–40%)**
  - Cash and cash equivalents, bank reconciliations.
  - Trade receivables and CECL (Current Expected Credit Losses - ASC 326).
  - Inventory valuation (FIFO, LIFO, Weighted Average, lower of cost or NRV).
  - Property, Plant, and Equipment (depreciation, impairment ASC 360).
  - Investments in debt and equity securities (ASC 320).
  - Intangible assets and goodwill impairment testing.
- **Area III: Select Transactions (20–30%)**
  - Leases (ASC 842): Operating vs. Finance leases for lessees and lessors.
  - Revenue Recognition (ASC 606): 5-Step Model.
  - Income taxes (ASC 740): Deferred tax assets/liabilities and valuation allowances.
  - Stockholders' equity, treasury stock, and earnings per share (Basic and Diluted EPS).
- **Area IV: State and Local Governments (5–15%)**
  - GASB standards, fund accounting (Governmental, Proprietary, Fiduciary funds).
  - Modified accrual vs. full accrual accounting; government-wide financial statements.

---

### 3. REG: Taxation and Regulation (Core)
*Focus: Federal tax compliance, ethics and professional responsibilities, business law fundamentals, individual taxation, and standard entity taxation.*
- **Area I: Ethics, Professional Responsibilities and Federal Tax Procedures (10–20%)**
  - Treasury Department Circular 230, AICPA Statements on Standards for Tax Services (SSTS).
  - Internal Revenue Code (IRC) civil penalties, audits, appeals, and statute of limitations.
- **Area II: Business Law (10–20%)**
  - Contract law (offer, acceptance, consideration, Statute of Frauds, breach).
  - Agency law (authority, fiduciary duties, liability).
  - Uniform Commercial Code (UCC Sales - Article 2) and Secured Transactions (Article 9).
  - Debtor-creditor relationships and bankruptcy (Chapter 7, Chapter 11).
- **Area III: Federal Taxation of Property Transactions (12–22%)**
  - Cost basis, adjusted basis, capital gains and losses (IRC Section 1221).
  - Depreciation conventions (MACRS, Section 179 expensing, bonus depreciation).
  - Like-kind exchanges (Section 1031) and involuntary conversions (Section 1033).
- **Area IV: Federal Taxation of Individuals (22–32%)**
  - Gross income inclusions and exclusions.
  - Adjustments for Adjusted Gross Income (AGI), Standard vs. Itemized deductions.
  - Qualified Business Income (QBI) deduction (Section 199A fundamentals).
  - Individual tax credits and Alternative Minimum Tax (AMT).
- **Area V: Federal Taxation of Entities (28–38%)**
  - C Corporations: Form 1120, taxable income, dividends received deduction (DRD), Schedule M-1/M-3 book-tax reconciliations.
  - S Corporations: Form 1120-S, eligibility, basis calculations, pass-through items.
  - Partnerships: Form 1065, inside vs. outside basis, guaranteed payments.

---

## 3.4 The 3 Discipline Sections (Detailed Blueprints)

Candidates choose **one** discipline that aligns with their career aspirations. A CPA licensed via any discipline receives the exact same, unrestricted CPA license.

### Choice A: BAR (Business Analysis and Reporting)
*For candidates pursuing careers in corporate finance, financial planning & analysis (FP&A), controller roles, technical accounting advisory, or government audit.*
- **Area I: Business Analysis (40–50%)**
  - Financial statement ratio analysis, trend evaluation, and performance metrics.
  - Capital structure optimization, working capital management, and cost of capital (WACC).
  - Budgeting, forecasting, variance analysis, and cost-volume-profit (CVP) analysis.
  - Enterprise risk management (ERM) and non-financial performance indicators (ESG metrics).
- **Area II: Technical Accounting and Reporting (35–45%)**
  - Advanced revenue recognition (software licenses, multi-element contracts).
  - Complex lease accounting (sale-leasebacks, lease modifications).
  - Business combinations and consolidations (ASC 805 / ASC 810), variable interest entities (VIEs), goodwill, and non-controlling interests.
  - Derivative instruments and hedge accounting (ASC 815).
  - Foreign currency transactions and translation (ASC 830).
  - Employee benefit plans and stock-based compensation (ASC 718).
- **Area III: State and Local Governments (10–20%)**
  - Advanced GASB accounting, Comprehensive Annual Financial Reports (ACFR/CAFR), derivation of government-wide financials from fund records.

---

### Choice B: ISC (Information Systems and Controls)
*For candidates pursuing careers in IT audit, SOC assurance, cybersecurity governance, forensic technology, and risk advisory.*
- **Area I: Information Systems and Data Governance (35–45%)**
  - IT Governance frameworks (COBIT, COSO, ITIL) and alignment with enterprise strategy.
  - Data governance, data architecture, master data management, and data lifecycle.
  - Privacy regulations and frameworks (GDPR, CCPA/CPRA, HIPAA).
  - System development methodologies (SDLC, Agile, DevSecOps) and change management.
- **Area II: Information Security and Network Architecture (35–45%)**
  - Network security, firewalls, IDS/IPS, zero-trust network access, and endpoint protection.
  - Identity and Access Management (IAM), RBAC, privileged access, and multi-factor authentication.
  - Cryptography, encryption at rest/transit, key management, and digital certificates.
  - Incident response, business continuity planning (BCP), and disaster recovery (DRP).
  - Cloud architectures (IaaS, PaaS, SaaS) and third-party risk management (TPRM).
- **Area III: System and Organization Controls (SOC) Engagements (15–25%)**
  - AICPA Statement on Standards for Attestation Engagements (SSAE No. 21/22).
  - SOC 1 (Type 1 vs. Type 2): Focus on Internal Controls over Financial Reporting (ICFR).
  - SOC 2 (Type 1 vs. Type 2): Focus on the Trust Services Criteria (Security, Availability, Processing Integrity, Confidentiality, Privacy).
  - SOC 3: General use summary reports.
  - Testing vendor controls, user entity controls (UECs), and subservice organization carved-out vs. inclusive methods.

---

### Choice C: TCP (Tax Compliance and Planning)
*For candidates pursuing careers in high-net-worth tax advisory, family offices, corporate tax departments, M&A tax, and international tax planning.*
- **Area I: Tax Compliance and Planning for Individuals and Personal Financial Planning (30–40%)**
  - Advanced individual tax planning, gross income planning, and timing of deductions.
  - Passive activity losses, at-risk limitations, and excess business losses.
  - Wealth transfer planning: Gift tax (Form 709), estate tax (Form 706), generation-skipping transfer (GST) tax.
  - Trust and estate income taxation (Form 1041), distributable net income (DNI).
  - Executive compensation planning (stock options: ISOs vs. NQSOs, restricted stock units).
- **Area II: Entity Tax Compliance (30–40%)**
  - C Corporation consolidated tax returns, net operating loss (NOL) utilization limitations (IRC Section 382).
  - S Corporation complex shareholder basis tracking, AAA accounts, and built-in gains tax.
  - Partnership distributions (proportionate vs. disproportionate), Section 754 elections, and Section 704(c) allocations.
  - Tax-exempt organizations: Form 990, unrelated business taxable income (UBTI).
- **Area III: Entity Tax Planning (20–30%)**
  - Choice of entity considerations and tax implications of business formation.
  - Corporate liquidations, redemptions, and tax-free reorganizations (Section 368).
  - Multi-jurisdictional and state/local tax (SALT) planning: Nexus, apportionment, and pass-through entity taxes (PTET).
  - International tax fundamentals: Subpart F income, GILTI, FDII, and foreign tax credits.
- **Area IV: Property Transactions (10–20%)**
  - Advanced depreciation recapture (Section 1245, 1250, unrecaptured 1250 gain).
  - Installment sales (Section 453) and related-party transaction rules (Section 267).

## 3.5 Scoring Mechanics, Rolling Window, and Licensure (The 4 Es)
- **Scoring & Pass Mark:**
  - Scaled score from **0 to 99**. A score of **75 or higher** is required to pass each section.
  - Scores are not percentage correct; psychometrically scaled through Item Response Theory (IRT).
- **Rolling Credit Window (30 Months):**
  - Under updated NASBA Uniform Accountancy Act (UAA) Model Rule 5-7, once a candidate passes their first section, they have a **30-month rolling credit window** (extended from the historical 18 months) to pass the remaining three sections.
  - The 30-month clock begins on the date scores are officially released for the first passed section.
  - *(Note: All unexpired exam credits held as of January 1, 2024, were granted an automatic bridge extension through June 30, 2025).*
- **The 4 Es of Licensure:**
  1. **Education:** 150 semester hours (typically a bachelor's + 30 master's/post-grad credits), including mandated minimums in accounting (usually 24–30 credits) and business administration.
  2. **Examination:** Passing all 4 sections (AUD, FAR, REG, + 1 Discipline) within the 30-month window.
  3. **Experience:** 1 to 2 years of verified professional experience in accounting, audit, tax, or advisory supervised and certified by an actively licensed CPA.
  4. **Ethics:** Passing the state-mandated ethics exam (e.g., AICPA Professional Ethics for CPAs with 90%+ pass benchmark).

## 5.4 Cross-Credential Strategic Synergies for Candidates
For ambitious finance and assurance professionals, these three designations offer unprecedented career synergy:
1. **The Modern Tech-Auditor Bridge (CPA + CISA):**
   - Combining the **CPA (ISC Discipline)** with the **ISACA CISA** creates an elite IT Assurance profile capable of leading Big 4 SOC 1, SOC 2, and cybersecurity risk engagements with dual statutory and systems credibility.
2. **The Islamic Financial Assurance Bridge (CPA / CA + CSAA):**
   - A Chartered Accountant or CPA who achieves the **AAOIFI CSAA** holds unrivaled global authority to serve as an external Shari'ah auditor or Shari'ah Supervisory Board consultant across sovereign wealth funds, Sukuk issuing syndicates, and Islamic financial institutions in the GCC and Southeast Asia.
3. **The Complete Tri-Credential Professional:**
   - Possessing CISA, CSAA, and CPA Evolution credentials represents the absolute pinnacle of global assurance: combining statutory financial audit authority, high-tech cybersecurity audit capability, and cross-border ethical/Islamic financial compliance.
